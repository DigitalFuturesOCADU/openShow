Decoding Origins is a research project that employs West African historical manuscripts including generated in the early nineteenth century in Sierra Leone, to discover unique identifiers of African pre-slavery identities. Records of manumitted enslaved individuals include visual descriptions of body markings, including intricate tattoos and scarification that can be used to specifically identify societies and branches of a given family. We strive to collect, analyze and present this data, in conjunction with culturally sensitive user design, ethno-linguistic and visual analysis, to extract meaningful information from these invaluable sources. 
	Decoding Origins uses accessible design and deployment of visual databases and interfaces which facilitate storage, access, and analysis of this textual and visual information, by scholars and the public alike. We are developing, training, and testing machine learning-based virtual mathematical models that are capable of cross-referencing body marks from the pilot datasets with all known patterns in the database, powering the likelihood of identifying kinship and birthplace. Research work to date is showing us that this approach will shed new light on demographic change, individual identities, and patterns of slave-taking. The new interfaces we have designed, giving accessible access to the data visualizations and the database, will become tools to study familial history as we further refine them. The ultimate goals of this project are to respect the provenance of important historical and personal data, and to use design, data gathering, analytics and visualization to join with ongoing efforts to restore and recover African identities which the slave trade sought to erase.
